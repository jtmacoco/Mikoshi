## Problem

You are given an integer array `nums`.

- The **low** score of `nums` is the **minimum** absolute difference between any two integers.
- The **high** score of `nums` is the **maximum** absolute difference between any two integers.
- The **score** of `nums` is the sum of the **high** and **low** scores.

Return the **minimum score** after **changing two elements** of `nums`.

---
# Example 1

**Input:** `nums = [1, 4, 7, 8, 5]`

**Output:** 3

**Explanation:**

- Change `nums[0]` and `nums[1]` to be 6 so that `nums` becomes [6,6,7,8,5].
- The low score is the minimum absolute difference: |6 - 6| = 0.
- The high score is the maximum absolute difference: |8 - 5| = 3.
- The sum of high and low score is 3.

---
# Solution

>[!important] Intuition
>- We get the min for free 
>- Sort list
>- Once sorted only change the ends really effect the output since nothing in the middle actually effects the output since only care about max and min 

Let’s go through it slowly with your array. Cross out the two elements you’re changing, then look at what’s left.

```
index:   0  1  2  3  4
value:   1  4  5  7  8
```

**Case 1: change the two smallest (indexes 0 and 1)**

```
index:   0  1  2  3  4
value:   X  X  5  7  8
```

- The smallest left is 5, at **index 2**.
- The largest left is 8, at **index 4** (also `-1`).
- High score = `nums[-1] - nums[2]` = 8 − 5 = 3.

**Case 2: change the two largest (indexes 3 and 4)**

```
index:   0  1  2  3  4
value:   1  4  5  X  X
```

- The smallest left is 1, at **index 0**.
- The largest left is 5, at **index 2** (also `-3`).
- High score = `nums[-3] - nums[0]` = 5 − 1 = 4.

**Case 3: change one from each end (indexes 0 and 4)**

```
index:   0  1  2  3  4
value:   X  4  5  7  X
```

```python
class Solution:
    def minimizeSum(self, nums: List[int]) -> int:
        nums.sort()
        left = abs( nums[-1] - nums[2] )
        right = abs( nums[-3] - nums[0] )
        mid = abs ( nums[1] - nums[-2] )
        return min(left,right,mid)
```
